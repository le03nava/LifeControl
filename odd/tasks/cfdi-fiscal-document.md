## Objective

Give the sale a fiscal document of its own — CFDI 4.0, stamped by a provider behind a port — with the issuer's and receiver's fiscal identities as first-class profiles, the authority's keys mapped once onto the tax vocabulary that already exists, and every fact the document states frozen on it at stamp time.

## Problem — measured in the tree, not quoted from the ADR

| Fact | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| No fiscal document exists anywhere | Zero matches for timbre, sello, certificado, régimen, uso CFDI, forma or método de pago, in migrations, Java and the Angular app |
| The money an invoice states already exists | The document carries its four parts and its currency under a constraint; each line carries a frozen `tax_code`, `tax_rate` and a derived `tax_amount` |
| The issuer is half fiscal | `companies` has `rfc`, `razon_social` and an address, and nothing that says its regime; `tipo_persona_id` is a bare integer with no catalog, no foreign key and no reader |
| The receiver is less | `customers.rfc` is nullable, unvalidated, and is the only fiscal column that exists |
| Three of the authority's keys are already columns | The measure unit's key is required and unique, the category's is constrained to eight digits, and the product's is twenty characters of free text with no check and no catalog |
| The concept's key is never copied to the line | The line snapshots the product's name and the variant's name, and not the fiscal key — the omission that makes a reprint read today's catalog against last year's sale |
| One tax per line, and no factor | `tax_code`, `tax_rate` and `tax_amount` are scalars; `EXENTO` and `TASA_0` differ only by a string in a code |
| The tree can only express a sale paid when it happens | The charge refuses unless the payments cover the total exactly, and a zero total is refused |

## Specs

**S1** — The issuer states its fiscal regime and its place of issue: `companies` gains a regime with a closed vocabulary and a postal code, both required to issue and neither required to sell.

**S2** — The receiver carries a fiscal profile, not a name and an identifier: legal name, regime, fiscal address with a postal code, and a default use — and `rfc`, when present, is validated for shape. The profile is required **at issue**, not at the till: a cash sale needs none and an invoice cannot exist without one.

**S3** — The tax vocabulary gains the authority's two coordinates as mapped columns on `tax_code` — `sat_impuesto` and `sat_tipo_factor` — rather than a parallel catalog. With them, a zero rate, an exempt item and a not-applicable one stop being the same row distinguished by a string, which is the first time this tree can tell `EXENTO` from `TASA_0` in a document.

**S4** — The product's fiscal key is constrained, not free text: a format check with the shape the category's key already has, in the same slice that starts reading it. The concept is the one element a stamp cannot omit, and it is the weakest fiscal column in the tree.

**S5** — The document is a row set of its own — `fiscal_document`, `fiscal_document_line`, `fiscal_document_artifact` — referencing the sale by `(source_type, source_id)`, never a column on the order. Every tenant table carries `company_id` and is armed; the two artefact tables are append-only on the pair `V83` established.

**S6** — At most one live stamped document per sale: a partial unique index over `(company_id, source_type, source_id)` where the status is the stamped one. A cancelled document and its replacement are two rows, and the pair is legal.

**S7** — A stamped document's facts cannot be updated or deleted, and its UUID is unique and non-null on a stamped row. Cancellation is a new row naming the motive and, for a substitution, the folio that replaces it.

**S8** — The invoice's line amounts equal the sale line's within one minor unit, checked by a constraint trigger **at stamp time**. It is the only place two documents are compared, and it is what keeps a deliberate duplication honest.

**S9** — Every fact the document states is frozen on it: key of product, key of unit, description, quantity, unit value, discount, tax object, base, tax, factor and rate — plus the receiver's identity as it was. Nothing is read from the live catalogs when a document is re-issued, reprinted or cancelled.

**S10** — The artefact returns the bytes that were stamped: a reprint reads the stored XML and never regenerates it, because a regenerated document cannot be proven to be the original.

**S11** — A stamp attempt is idempotent: the same attempt never stamps twice, and a failure leaves no stamped row.

**S12** — The document declares its type, its place of issue and its export status as columns with a closed vocabulary, because two of the three are required on every document by the schema's fourth revision. Catalog **values** are read from the version in force at stamp time and are never pinned in code or in the ADR.

**S13** — Only a sale paid when it happens can be invoiced. The payment form and the payment method are conditional in the authority's schema and the deferred method is what forces a placeholder plus a payment complement, so the deferred method is refused as a validation — named, not half-modelled.

**S14** — The fiscal document never creates revenue. ADR-0008 posts from the sale, so a stamp in a later month, a cancellation, or a sale never invoiced all leave the books describing what happened commercially.

(a) **The regime vocabulary moves to a `fiscal` package** and `UsoCfdi` joins it there, because this slice is the first second user: two copies of `c_RegimenFiscal`, one for the issuer and one for the receiver, would be two copies free to drift, and a customer importing `company.model` would say the vocabulary belongs to the issuer. (c) **`fiscal_zip_code` is `VARCHAR(5)`**, not the `VARCHAR(10)` the ADR's schema sketch gives the receiver, for the reason the issuer's amendment already states: the fact is five digits, and a ten-character column invites a postal code with a hyphen that no stamp accepts. (d) **The shape of `rfc` is the tree's existing `@ValidRFC`** (`A-ZÑ&{3,4}\d{6}[A-Za-z0-9]{3}`), the validator `CompanyRequest` already uses — not a second regex that could disagree with it. The alternative refused is a half profile waiting for the document to refuse it: it looks like a profile in every listing and cannot be used in one. `fiscal_street`, `fiscal_state` and `fiscal_country_id` are carried and optional, because what a stamp states of the receiver's domicile is the postal code. **L9 (2026-11-14) — A2 decided before the code: the document's lifecycle and its frozen facts, and the four gaps the ADR's schema block leaves for T4 and T5.** (a) **Two migrations, one per slice, and the split is the task titles verbatim.** `V108` (T4) creates the three tables with the columns that make a document *exist as a history* — identity, state, the authority's UUID, the cancellation pointers — and arms them; `V109` (T5) adds every column that says *what the document states* and the trigger that keeps it honest against the sale. Nothing can stamp between the two: no writer exists until A3, so a table that cannot yet hold a complete document is a state no caller can reach, and `V109`'s `NOT NULL` additions land on empty tables.

(b) **The state machine is `DRAFT | STAMPED | CANCELLED`, and the append-only pair is scoped so the machine can actually move.** `DELETE` is revoked from `lifecontrol_app` on all three tables; `UPDATE` is revoked on `fiscal_document_line` and `fiscal_document_artifact`, which are written once and never touched again, and **granted** on `fiscal_document`, because the one legitimate update is the transition out of `DRAFT` into `STAMPED`. A bare `UPDATE`/`DELETE` against a row that is already `STAMPED` or `CANCELLED` raises, so the grant cannot rewrite a witnessed document, and the identity columns (`company_id`, `source_type`, `source_id`) are refused on change on any row: moving a document to another sale is not a transition. A `DRAFT` is therefore the only mutable state, and a failed attempt leaves a draft rather than garbage nobody can delete — which is why purging abandoned drafts is not in this slice.

(c) **A cancellation is a row, and the ADR's block was missing the pointer that makes it one.** Decision 3 says the cancellation *is a new row naming the cancelled document*; the block only carries `replaces_fiscal_document_id`, which points the other way. `cancels_fiscal_document_id` joins it, with a composite foreign key `(company_id, cancels_fiscal_document_id) → fiscal_document (company_id, id)` — the same-tenant pointer rule `V106` established — and a unique index, because a document is cancelled once. A named `CHECK` couples the three: a row that names a cancelled document is `CANCELLED`, carries a `cancellation_motive`, and carries no `uuid_fiscal` of its own, because the UUID the authority cancels belongs to the cancelled row. A `replaces_fiscal_document_id` is legal only on such a row. Which motives exist is the authority's catalog, and decision 11 keeps it out of the schema: the column is two characters and its vocabulary lands with the first writer.

(d) **`fiscal_document_artifact` is one row per document, and that is what makes S10 enforceable.** The ADR gives it the document's id as its primary key, and the reading is kept deliberately: two artefacts for one document would be two candidate originals and no way to say which one the authority witnessed. A replacement document gets its own artefact. The table is insert-only by trigger and by revoked privileges.

(e) **`fiscal_document_line.tax_code` is `VARCHAR(20)`, not the block's `VARCHAR(3)`.** The column copies the tree's internal code — `NA`, `IVA`, `TASA_0`, `EXENTO` — from `sales_order_items.tax_code`, and `EXENTO` does not fit in three characters. The block was written before the copy it describes.

(f) **The issuer's identity is frozen too, and the block froze only the receiver's.** Decision 4 names the receiver; `S9` states the blanket rule — every fact the document states is frozen on it. A document states the issuer's `rfc`, legal name and regime exactly as it states the receiver's, and `companies.rfc` is a mutable column, so a document that read it live would change its face the day a company corrects its name. `emisor_rfc`, `emisor_name` and `emisor_regimen` join `lugar_expedicion`, which the block already had. This is the third amendment to the same schema block, and the same reason as the first two: the block was written before the tree held the fact.

(g) **No `rounding_adjustment` on the document, and that is precisely why `S8` tolerates one minor unit.** `sales_orders` carries `rounding_adjustment` and `ck_order_total` makes its four parts add up; the authority's document states subtotal, discount and tax, and its total is their sum. Copying the rounding would put on the invoice a number the authority does not accept, and dropping it makes the two totals disagree by at most the rounding `ck_order_rounding_bounded` already bounds to one minor unit — the tolerance `S8` grants. So the comparison is `abs(document − sale) <= one minor unit` of the document's currency, taken from `currency.minor_units` and never from a literal.

(h) **The comparison is one deferred constraint trigger on `fiscal_document`, not one per line.** It fires `AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE … WHEN (NEW.status = 'STAMPED')`, `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`, so a stamp that writes the document and its lines in one transaction is judged once, at commit, instead of at each row. It compares every line with the `sales_order_item` it names — quantity, unit value, discount and tax — and the document's three totals with the sale's, raising with the line and both amounts named. Lines reach the sale item by `sales_order_item_id`, which is `NOT NULL`: an invoice line that names no sale line is a line nobody could compare, and `S8` exists to make the comparison possible.

(i) **The vocabularies are `CHECK`s now; the enums arrive with their first reader.** `L6`(c) and `L8`(b) established the enum mirrored by a named `CHECK` with a parity test, and this slice deliberately does not add five enums that nothing reads yet — the audit in `docs/` already flags unused code. `fiscal_document.status`, `tipo_comprobante`, `metodo_pago`, `exportacion` and `cancellation_motive` are named `CHECK`s asserted against `pg_constraint` by the migration test, so each closed vocabulary is verified where it lives; A3's builder, the first reader, brings the enums and the parity test with it.

(j) **Every new table gets the migration's own self-check**, the block `V97`, `V99`, `V83` and `V105` already copy: `ENABLE` plus `FORCE`, exactly one `p_tenant_<table>` policy, and the `tenant_table` row asserted to read `tenant`/`company_id` — because `TenantIsolationStartupCheck` refuses to boot a database where `unprotected_tenant_tables()` is non-empty, and a table created without that row turns a migration omission into a boot failure in every environment at once.

**L11 (2026-11-14) — A2's second half, and the one place `L9` was wrong.** `V109` adds what the document states and the comparison that keeps it honest (`S8`, `S9`); its suite builds a real sale and pins eight cases, and the previous slice's suite was adapted rather than weakened. Six decisions and one correction.

(a) **`L9`(i) overreached, and this slice reverses it for three columns.** `exportacion`, `tax_object` and `cancellation_motive` carry **no** `CHECK`: their value sets are `c_Exportacion`, `c_ObjetoImp` and `c_MotivoCancelacion`, none of which this tree states anywhere, and ADR-0007 decision 11 says the shape of a fiscal document is a fact to look up and never one to recall. Writing `'01'…'04'` from memory would be exactly the failure that decision exists to prevent — and a wrong closed vocabulary is worse than none, because it refuses a legal document and accepts a wrong one. The migration's self-check and a test case both assert the **absence** of those `CHECK`s, so a later edit cannot quietly pin a catalog. The enums and their vocabularies arrive with A3's builder, together.

(b) **The comparison is one deferred constraint trigger on the document** (`L9`(h)), and it deliberately does **not** copy `V93`'s row lock: nothing here aggregates another writer's rows — the lines are append-only and the five amounts per line come from the row the same transaction wrote — and the sale is read, never written, so a lock would serialize a stamp against an edit it does not touch and would not make the comparison truer. A sale edited *after* the stamp is precisely what a snapshot exists to survive.

(c) **One condition beyond `S8`'s letter: a line of another sale is refused.** Neither pointer can say it alone — the document names the sale, the line names the sale line, and the pair is complete only inside the trigger. Without it, a document could state a line whose amounts agree with a sale it has nothing to do with, and every comparison in this slice would pass.

(d) **What the trigger does not check is named in the migration rather than discovered later**: it does not verify that the document's `subtotal` is the sum of its own lines. A stamped document whose totals equal the sale's while its line set is incomplete would pass. That is the builder's obligation in A3, and a second definition of the document's arithmetic beside `ck_fiscal_document_total` belongs to the slice that has a writer to hold to it.

(e) **The tolerance is one minor unit of the document's own currency**, read from `currency.minor_units` and never from a literal (`L9`(g)). The limit is stated: the boundary is asserted on both sides for `MXN`, and no case exercises a currency with a different scale, so the derivation is provable from the code and pinned at one point.

(f) **The chain's second slice adapted the first, and that is the chain working rather than churn.** `V109`'s `NOT NULL` columns invalidated the previous suite's fixtures and its `STAMPED` rows named random sales that the new trigger refuses — correctly, for a reason unrelated to each rule it pins. So that suite now states the columns its sibling requires, and the company/store/product/variant/sale chain both suites build lives once, in `support/SaleFixtures`: two copies of a fixture that must satisfy a `NOT NULL` set and a commit-time trigger is two copies free to disagree. No assertion of the previous slice was dropped, relaxed or restated beyond naming a real sale where it stamps.

(g) **Size, again stated rather than discovered**: `1685` authored lines, past the ~400 budget, and not split for the reason `L10` gives — one migration is one story, and a `V109` that added half the columns would leave a document that is neither frozen nor comparable. `T4` and `T5` are the two pull requests this issue's chain declares, and this closes it.

**L12 (2026-11-15) — A3 decided from the authority's schema and the tree's own conventions, both looked up.** `T6` is the stamping port, the CFDI builder and the idempotent attempt. Ten decisions, and the tenth is why this slice is two pull requests.

(a) **The interface is `FiscalStampProvider`, not `*Port`.** The tree has exactly one outbound boundary and it is named `IdentityProvider` with `KeycloakIdentityProvider` behind it; `Port` and `Adapter` appear zero times in `src/main`. The ADR's word stays a concept, the code follows the tree, and `T7`'s adapter will be `<Vendor>FiscalStampProvider` registered behind `@ConditionalOnProperty` the way `ProvisioningWorkerSchedulerConfig` does it.

(b) **The seal is an input, never the builder's job.** `Sello`, `NoCertificado` and `Certificado` are **required** on `cfdi:Comprobante` (XSD 4.0), and producing them is certificate handling — `T7`'s subject. The builder takes an `IssuerSeal` record and writes the three attributes; a test passes a fixed one, and no crypto enters this slice.

(c) **`Fecha` carries no timezone, and this tree has no company timezone.** The XSD pattern forbids an offset and the guide says it is the local time where the document is issued. So the attempt renders the build instant in **one configured zone** (`app.fiscal.timezone`, default `America/Mexico_City`, the shape every other `@ConfigurationProperties` record here has), and the limit is named instead of hidden: a company in another zone needs its own, which is a column this slice does not add. The builder takes the already-rendered `LocalDateTime` so its output is deterministic in tests.

(d) **Decimals come from the catalog, never from a literal.** The concept's `Importe` allows six decimals and our money is `NUMERIC(19,4)`; the document's `SubTotal`, `Descuento` and `Total` are rounded to the **currency's** decimals, read from `currency.minor_units` exactly as `SalesOrderService` already does. `TasaOCuota` is six decimals.

(e) **`ObjetoImp` is frozen on the row and derived once, at snapshot time.** `NA` means not subject → `01`; every other code is subject and does break its tax out → `02`. Codes `01`–`03` are confirmed by the guide; `04` exists only in a secondary mirror, so nothing writes it. The builder **writes what the row says** — the derivation belongs to the snapshot writer, which is the only place a live catalog is read, and the last place it ever is.

(f) **An exempt concept carries no `TasaOCuota` and no `Importe`.** `TipoFactor` is `Exento`, and the guide states it explicitly; a `Tasa` line carries both. This is why `tipo_factor` was frozen as a column instead of being re-derived.

(g) **The port's response has two shapes, not one.** A provider answers a stamped document (RFC of the certificate authority, UUID, stamp date, the two sellos, the certified serial, the signed XML) **or** a rejection with a numbered list of incidencias — a PAC's error catalogue is the operator's only way to fix the request, so a rejection is a value the caller inspects, not an exception that swallows the codes.

(h) **Idempotency is schema, not a service habit.** `F9` says the attempt key is unique per sale, and the tree's precedent is exact: `account_entry`'s `UNIQUE (company_id, source_type, source_id, posting)` with `ON CONFLICT … DO NOTHING RETURNING id` in `V100`. `V110` adds the attempt key to `fiscal_document`, unique per `(company_id, source_type, source_id)`, so a replayed attempt finds its own key and writes nothing.

(i) **`T6` ships as two pull requests on one issue.** The first is the snapshot writer and the attempt key: sale → document + lines, `DRAFT`, with `V110`. The second is the port, the builder and the attempt that stamps. The reason is not taste — the first is the only code that reads a live catalog and freezes it, and it must be reviewable on its own before a builder starts trusting its rows.

(j) **What the lookup did not settle is recorded as unsettled, not filled.** The SAT's catalog workbook could not be parsed, so `c_Exportacion`'s full set and `c_ObjetoImp`'s `04` are not pinned; no literal PAC response body was retrieved, so `T7`'s DTO is **unconfirmed** until the chosen vendor's reference is read; and `c_MotivoCancelacion`'s values `01`–`04` plus the rule that `FolioSustitucion` is required for `01` **are** confirmed by the XSD enumeration, which is the input `T8` will want and this slice does not apply.

**L13 (2026-11-15) — the first review of `T6` found a row telling a lie, and the fix is a schema change.** `V109` made `fiscal_document_line.tipo_factor` `NOT NULL` over `Tasa`, `Cuota` and `Exento`, which fits every *subject* line and forced a *not-subject* one — `tax_code = NA`, `tax_object = '01'` — to borrow `Exento`. The first implementation did exactly that and argued it well: of the three values it is the one whose own rule is “a factor and no rate”, and `tax_object` is what the second half's builder reads to emit no tax node.

That is not good enough for a row a stamp freezes. A concept with `ObjetoImp="01"` carries no `Traslado` and therefore **no `TipoFactor`** in the authority's schema, and `Exento` is the one value that means *subject and exempt* — a frozen row would be asserting that about an item that is subject to nothing. It is the same failure `V103` existed to remove between `EXENTO` and `TASA_0`, one level down: two different facts collapsed into one word.

So `V110` relaxes the column and couples it to the object in both directions, with `ck_fiscal_document_line_factor_matches_object CHECK ((tax_object = '01') = (tipo_factor IS NULL))`: a not-subject line states `01` and **no** factor, a subject line states `02`/`03` and **does** state one, and neither half can be true alone. `V109`'s vocabulary `CHECK` is untouched — `IN (…)` over `NULL` is `NULL`, which a `CHECK` accepts — and the suite that freezes `V109`'s `CHECK` inventory gained the coupling as a deliberate entry, which is the mechanism working: a constraint that changes the shape of a line has to be named in a list, not discovered.

The lesson worth keeping: the schema of `V109` was written from the ADR's sketch and from `L12`(f), and both described the *exempt* case while neither described the *not-subject* one. A model that has a column for “the factor” and no word for “there is no factor” will fill it with the least-wrong word available, and a least-wrong word in an immutable row is a wrong one.

**L14 (2026-11-15) — the second half of `T6`, and the three facts it leaves outside the row set on purpose.** The port, the builder and the attempt are in; four decisions are worth the ink, and the fourth is a gap that `T7` has to inherit knowingly.

(a) **The port's answer is a sealed two: `Stamped` or `Rejected`.** The first carries the provider's own `xml` — the bytes the authority witnessed, which are what the artefact stores — alongside the `uuid` that becomes `uuid_fiscal`. The second carries the provider's numbered issues, because that catalogue is the only thing an operator can act on. An exception for a rejection would throw away the codes.

(b) **The attempt refuses before it calls.** Replay first, then “this sale already has a live stamped document”, and only then the draft and the network. The order is not cosmetic: `uq_fiscal_document_one_stamped` would refuse the second row **after** the authority had already stamped a document this transaction is about to lose, and an orphan timbre is a document that exists at the authority and nowhere in this tree.

(c) **The artefact and the transition are one transaction**, so a committed `STAMPED` row always has its XML and a rolled-back attempt leaves neither. `FiscalDocument` gained the two setters the transition needs, and its javadoc says what actually protects the row afterwards: `V108`'s guard trigger, not the mapping.

(d) **Two facts do not fit the row set, and this slice does not invent a migration for them.** `Impuesto` (the SAT code) is frozen **into the XML** but has no column: `S9`'s list of frozen facts does not name it, `fiscal_document_line` keeps the tree's `tax_code` instead, and the builder therefore receives the mapping as an argument and stays pure. The artefact is the authority's copy, so a reprint is exact; a rebuild from rows alone would need the catalogue, and nothing needs that. And the provider's `rfcProvCertif`, `noCertificadoSat`, `selloCfd` and `selloSat` have no columns either — they live **inside** the artefact XML, byte for byte. `T8`'s cancellation reads them from there; if a later slice needs them queryable, that is one migration with a stated reason, not a guess made here.

(e) **No provider bean exists until `T7`, and that absence is a named refusal, not a boot failure.** The stamper injects an `ObjectProvider<FiscalStampProvider>` and refuses with `FiscalStampProviderNotConfiguredException`; a required bean would stop the whole API from starting for a capability nobody has configured yet, and a fallback bean would make `T7`'s registration order-dependent. The branch is untested for the reason that no context in this slice boots without a provider — `T7` is what tests it.

(f) **Size again**: `2309` authored lines in one pull request, the second of the two `L12`(i) declared. The split `T6` ships as is behavioural — “the sale is frozen” then “the document is stamped” — and not a line count.

**L15 (2026-11-15) — A4 splits at the provider line, and the half that does not need one lands first.** `T8` is written as depending on `T7`, and the dependency is real for exactly one of its three subjects: cancelling at the authority, substituting, and reprinting. The first two are calls with a contract and a failure mode; the third is a `SELECT`. So this slice delivers `S10`'s read path, the whole of `S13` and the whole of `S14`, and the cancelling half waits for a vendor. Six decisions.

(a) **The reprint reads the artefact and never the builder.** `S10` is explicit — a regenerated document cannot be proven to be the original — so the endpoint returns `fiscal_document_artifact.xml` byte for byte. `T6`'s `CfdiXmlBuilder` is a *stamping* input: it states what will be sealed, not what was. A path that built XML for a printed document would produce something that looks identical and is not the same bytes, and the difference is the whole point of keeping the artefact.

(b) **The bytes are served as bytes**: `application/xml` with an attachment disposition, asserted byte-identical against what the fake provider returned, whitespace and prolog included. Rendering a third-party fiscal document inline in a browser is an injection surface this tree does not need to open, and `text/plain` would invite someone to "fix" the document by hand.

(c) **Another company's document is a 404, not a 403, and the policy is why.** The row is invisible to the reader through `apply_tenant_isolation`, so "not found" is what the query answers; producing a 403 would need a second query outside the policy to learn that the document exists — which is the leak the policy exists to close. The same answer covers a `DRAFT` and a document whose stamp was rejected: neither has an artefact, and a reprint of one is a reprint of nothing.

(d) **The PUE-only refusal belongs to the writer, before its first write.** `S13` names the deferred method as refused **as a validation**, and the place where a sale becomes a document is `FiscalDocumentSnapshotWriter`. A refusal in the stamper would run after the draft existed, leaving a row to clean up in a table that refuses `DELETE` (`V108`); a refusal in the endpoint would leave the rule to whoever remembers to call it. So the writer checks `metodo_pago` first, names the method in the message, and writes nothing. The exception is its own type so the HTTP layer can map it to **400** without pattern-matching a string.

(e) **`S14` is pinned by reading the accounting entry set around a stamp, not by inspecting the poster.** The test takes a completed sale, records the entry ids and amounts the projection wrote, stamps its fiscal document with the fake provider, and asserts the set is identical. That is the whole of ADR-0008's seam in one assertion: the books describe the sale, and a document issued later — or never — moves nothing.

(f) **The schema does not move.** No new version for the *tables*: the columns these three rules need are all in `V108`–`V110`, and a migration that added a column nobody reads would be a renumbering. (`V111` does exist — it seeds the `FISCAL` activity process, which is data, not schema; see `L17`.)

**L16 (2026-11-15) — A4's provider-free half landed, and `S13` cannot be proven end to end for a reason worth writing down.** The reprint (`S10`), the PUE-only refusal (`S13`) and the books seam (`S14`) are in, with 184 tests green across the fiscal and accounting suites. Four decisions, one of them a gap.

(a) **`S13`'s refusal is wired and unexercised, and no fixture was invented to pretend otherwise.** `metodo_pago` reaches the document from `V109`'s `DEFAULT 'PUE'`, the writer never sets it, and no sale can state the deferred method: `V87` dropped `sales_orders.payment_method_id` and the charge refuses anything but an exact payment. So the rule is a predicate pinned at unit level plus a call site in the only path a sale takes to become a document — real, unskippable by a future caller, and **not** proven by a refusal. The alternative would have been a raw `UPDATE` of a column the writer does not write, which would have "proven" a state the application cannot reach. The gap closes the day a sale can carry its own payment method, and that is a different unit with its own specs.

(b) **The 400 for that refusal falls out of the parent type, not out of an edited handler.** `FiscalDeferredPaymentNotSupportedException` extends `IllegalArgumentException`, which `GlobalExceptionHandler` already answers with 400 — the precedent is `FiscalAttemptKeyRequiredException` in the same package. A unit test asserts the parent type, so a silent drift to 500 fails a test rather than surprising an operator.

(c) **The reprint states two filters of its own — `STAMPED` and `SALES_ORDER` — because the schema does not couple the artefact to the status.** The writer sets the status and writes the artefact in one transaction, so the reader is not a second source of truth: it refuses to serve what a reprint must not serve (a draft with a stray artefact row, a document whose stamp was rejected) and answers 404 for all of them, and for another company, because the tenant policy is what makes the row invisible.

(d) **The seam test anchors the accounting entry on the `status_transition`, not on the sale**, and the reason is a failure mode worth remembering: `V100`'s revenue entry names the transition as its source, so a query by `source_id = sale.id` returns zero rows — and "no entry moved" would then be true for the wrong reason, passing a test that proves nothing. The suite asserts the entry ids and amounts are identical around a stamp, by the source the poster actually writes.

**L17 (2026-11-15) — the first endpoint this unit adds found the border, and the local filter is what hid it.** `GET /api/fiscal-documents/{id}/xml` was green in the API and **unreachable in production**, because a route is two halves — the controller and the gateway's `.route(...)` — and the second half lives in another module. `GatewayRouteCoverageTests` caught it in CI: `/api/fiscal-documents` is a prefix the API exposes and the gateway does not proxy, so every request answered a gateway 404. The route is now declared beside `/api/sales-orders/**`, and the gateway suite is green locally.

Two things worth keeping. First, **the defect was invisible to the command that produced the evidence**: `./gradlew test --tests '*Fiscal*' --tests '*Accounting*'` compiles and runs the whole API, but the gateway is a separate build with its own suite, and a filtered run in one module can never see the other. A local check that says "184 green" is a statement about what it ran. Second, the same CI run failed the API's `Test` job for a reason that was **not** the code — Maven Central answered `429 Too Many Requests` while resolving `h2` and `spring-boot-starter-test`. An infrastructure refusal read like a red slice; it is not, and the distinction matters when deciding whether to change code or to re-run.

**The second run found the same rule with a second instance, one module and one layer away.** `ActivityLogIntegrationTest` derives a process name from every production controller's package segment — `fiscal.controller` → `FISCAL` — and `ActivityLogService.save` **discards** an entry whose process is absent from `activity_processes` with only a `WARN`. So the endpoint worked and its audit trail was thrown away silently. `V111` seeds the process, idempotently, in the shape `V4`, `V16`, `V27`, `V78`, `V91` and `V101` already use.

Put together, the two failures are one rule about this tree, and it is worth stating as a rule and not as two anecdotes: **a new endpoint is never only its own code.** It is also its route in the gateway and its process in the audit vocabulary, and everything else a future inventory guard decides to require. Neither was visible from a filtered suite, and both were caught by a test that discovers rather than lists — which is exactly why those guards scan the package tree instead of a hardcoded array. It is the fourth time the activity-process guard has caught this omission (`V78`, `V91`, `V101`, `V111`); "the guard was right again" is the cheapest sentence in this record and the most expensive one to ignore.

**L18 (2026-11-15) — the provider is decided, its contract is read from its own WSDL, and `T7` ships as two units for a reason the WSDL makes plain.** The maintainer's decision, in their words: «nos podemos ir con solucion factible, toma esa decision y la ejecutamos en la siguiente session». This entry records it together with the four consequences the contract imposes that `T6` could not have known, because three of them change code `T6` already wrote.

(a) **The provider is Solución Factible (`SFE0807172W8`), and the deciding axis is custody of the CSD.** We hold the private key; the PAC only validates the seal. The alternatives are recorded in `L17`'s comparison and unchanged: Facturama is the velocity alternative that takes the key (irreversible — only a SAT revocation undoes it) and takes JSON instead of XML, so `CfdiXmlBuilder` would be bypassed; Diverza's authorisation has been on the SAT's *sin efectos* list since 2018-12-27. The costs accepted are named and not softened: **SOAP**, so the tree grows its first SOAP call; **per-call credentials**, so no token cache; **quote-only pricing**; and the **signing pipeline is ours**, which is the second unit below and the real price of custody.

(b) **The contract was read, not recalled** — `https://testing.solucionfactible.com/ws/services/Timbrado?wsdl`, a public document. Test host `https://testing.solucionfactible.com/ws/services/Timbrado`, production `https://solucionfactible.com/ws/services/Timbrado`. SOAP 1.1, `style="document"`, `soapAction="urn:timbrarBase64"`, request element `<timbrarBase64>` in namespace `http://timbrado.ws.cfdi.solucionfactible.com` with children `usuario`, `password`, `cfdiBase64` (**`xs:base64Binary`**, not a string) and `ZIP` (`xs:boolean`). The answer is `<timbrarResponse><return>` of type `CFDICertificacion` in `…/xsd`: `status` (`xs:int`), `mensaje`, and `resultados[]` of `CFDIResultadoCertificacion` — `status`, `mensaje`, `uuid`, `versionTFD`, `fechaTimbrado` (`xs:dateTime`), `selloSAT`, `certificadoSAT`, `cadenaOriginal`, `cfdiTimbrado` (`xs:base64Binary`), `qrCode`. The `Timbrado` service also carries the `cancelar`, `cancelarPorNotaCredito`, `cancelarViaCSDAlmacenado` and `enviarSolicitudCancelacion` operations, which is `T8`'s input and confirms the cancelling half is reachable — `enviarSolicitudCancelacion` takes a base64 request, which is the operation that can carry a motive and a substituting folio.

(c) **`rfcProvCertif` and `selloCfd` are not in the answer: they are inside the timbre.** `FiscalStampResult.Stamped` requires six facts and the provider returns four of them as fields. `RfcProvCertif` and `SelloCFD` are attributes of the `TimbreFiscalDigital` **inside** the XML the provider returns in `cfdiTimbrado`, so the adapter decodes that base64 and reads them from the document it is handing over — which is not a workaround but the correct order: the timbre is the authority's statement, and a field beside it would be a second copy free to disagree. A returned XML with no `TimbreFiscalDigital`, or one whose `UUID` disagrees with `resultados[0].uuid`, is a provider defect the adapter refuses instead of storing.

(d) **A `Sello` cannot be computed from a document that already states it, and `T6`'s builder writes one.** `CfdiXmlBuilder` receives `IssuerSeal` whole (`L12`(b)) and emits `Sello`, `NoCertificado` and `Certificado`; but the seal is the signature **over** the document, so the bytes it is computed from carry `NoCertificado` and `Certificado` and **no** `Sello`. The amendment is one rule in the builder — it writes `Sello` only when the value is non-blank — and the pipeline renders twice from the **same** `LocalDateTime`: once seal-less, which the SAT's official XSLT turns into the `cadena original`, and once with the seal the signature produced. Two renderings that differ in one attribute are provable; a document patched by string replacement after the fact would be a signature over bytes nobody kept. The consequence for `T6`'s code is explicit: `FiscalDocumentStamper` stops receiving an `IssuerSeal` and asks the sealer for it, because the seal is a function of the bytes that class just built and a caller cannot know them.

(e) **`status = 200` is the call, not the document.** The response has two levels, and a successful call can carry a refused document: process `200` with `resultados[0].status = 302` is a seal the authority would not accept. The adapter therefore maps **the first result's status**, and an empty `resultados` on a process `200` is refused as a provider defect rather than reported as a stamp — the shape `L12`(g) gives the port (a rejection is a value with codes) survives, and a `200` with no result cannot become one. The catalogue is two-level and numeric (`601`–`605` authentication, `301`–`308` structure, seal and certificate, `401`–`405` dates and regime, `611`, `612`, `620`, `621` request shape, `630`–`633` exhausted timbres and fair use), and it travels in `Rejected.issues` untranslated, which is `L12`(g).

(f) **`T7` is two units, and the split is by dependency, not by size.** `T7a`, the adapter: the SOAP call behind `FiscalStampProvider`, its two-level status mapping, the timbre read of (c), a `@ConfigurationProperties` record with **no default credentials** (a missing one fails the context at boot, the `ProvisioningWorkerProperties` shape, never a silent fallback to the public test account), and `@ConditionalOnProperty` registration so the API still starts without a provider — which is what `T6`'s `ObjectProvider` refusal expects. `T7b`, the sealing pipeline: the SAT's official `cadenaoriginal_4_0.xslt` committed as a resource with its provenance, the transform, SHA-256 + RSA-SHA256, base64, and the CSD read from configuration. `T7b` depends on `T7a` only in that both are `T7`; the adapter is testable against a canned SF response and the sealer against a vector, so they ship in that order.

(g) **No SOAP library, and the reason is the review, not the bytes.** The call is one fixed envelope: a `<timbrarBase64>` with four children against one namespace, and a response whose shape the WSDL has not changed in fifteen years. `WebServiceTemplate` or CXF would add a marshaller, a binding generator or a build plugin to this tree's first — and only — SOAP call, and `T7a`'s tests would then be a test of the marshaller. A hand-built envelope over `java.net.http.HttpClient`, parsed with the `DocumentBuilderFactory` the builder's own suite already uses, adds **no dependency** and is pinned by the vendor's own published example response. The limit is stated: this client understands one operation and one response; `T8`'s cancellation is a second envelope with its own test, not a general SOAP stack.

(h) **The private key's format is stated as unverified where it is unverified.** A CSD `.key` as the SAT delivers it is a container this tree cannot open without adding a crypto provider, and no real CSD exists in this repository to prove a loader against. So the configuration takes the private key as **base64 PKCS#8** (optionally password-protected) and the certificate as **base64 DER**, the operator converts the SAT files once, and the record says that step is the operator's instead of pretending a `.key` reader was verified. The test vector is a generated RSA key pair and a fixed certificate fixture, so the seal is verified against a public key and `NoCertificado`/`Certificado` against bytes — an honest split of what is proven from what is only derived.

(i) **Open confirmations, none of them blocking either unit.** Current authorisation number and date; timbrado price at 500 and 2,000 documents a month; explicit confirmation that an **already sealed** XML is accepted without uploading the private key; and test-host access for CFDI 4.0. The fallback if the spike fails is Facturama, unchanged, with the custody cost `L18`(a) names.

**L19 (2026-11-15) — `T7` landed as the two units `L18` declared, and the four things only the implementation could tell.** The slice is `a350e678` (`L18`, the decision), `f2b2fde4` (`T7a`, the adapter) and `0fb8ea07` (`T7b`, the sealing pipeline), with `9f5ddcd4` carrying what the independent verification forced, on the two chained branches `feat/fiscal-provider` then `feat/fiscal-seal`. Both were cut from `origin/main @ 350c91fb` and rebased onto `57cde342` before review: `main` gained nine commits meanwhile, all in the Angular app, so nothing of this unit moved — and the rebase is the reason the record's hashes are not the ones `L18` was committed under.

(a) **The authority's stylesheet is a dependency, not a file.** `cadenaoriginal_4_0` declares `version="2.0"`, and the JDK's transformer supports 1.0 only — and does not fail cleanly on 2.0: it can process it with 1.0 semantics and answer a different `cadena original`, which is a signature the PAC refuses while everything looks correct. So `Saxon-HE 12.10` joins `build.gradle`, MPL-2.0 (file-level copyleft, no obligation on this tree's own sources), pulling `org.xmlresolver` and nothing else on `runtimeClasspath`: **5.96 MB** of runtime and **225 KB** of resources, both measured rather than estimated. This is the one new dependency `T7` takes, and it is the price of not writing the chain by hand — the failure `L12`(j) exists to prevent.

(b) **The stylesheet does not stand alone, and the 33 files next to it are not optional.** It resolves 33 `xsl:include` targets at compile time, so the set is committed verbatim beside it with each file's URL, byte count and SHA-256 in the README, and a test asserts the committed bytes hash to the documented value. The alternatives were refused on grounds that do not change with fashion: fetching at build time puts the network inside the build, and stubbing them changes the chain the authority published.

(c) **Saxon's security answers, recorded because they contradict what the JAXP names promise.** `FEATURE_SECURE_PROCESSING` is accepted. `ACCESS_EXTERNAL_DTD` is **refused** with an `IllegalArgumentException` — Saxon does not know the property. `ACCESS_EXTERNAL_STYLESHEET` is accepted by `setAttribute` and **ignored**: `getAttribute` still reports `all`. So the boundary is where the code puts it, the `URIResolver`, which accepts only the hrefs the committed stylesheet declares and refuses an `http`, a `file:`, a path or an undeclared name by name. The limit that stays, and is written in the javadoc rather than left to be found: a `DOCTYPE SYSTEM` naming an href **byte-identical** to one of the stylesheet's own declarations is answered with the committed file, because Saxon hands the resolver the same `href` for a real `xsl:include` and for a `DOCTYPE` and the two cannot be told apart there. Nothing local is read and nothing is fetched in that case — the substitution is a committed file — but it is a fact about the engine, not a proof about the resolver.

(d) **The independent verification found three things, and each is now closed by a test rather than by an argument.** (1) The transform's protection was incidental to the include allowlist: an external entity, a parameter entity and an external `DOCTYPE` all reached the resolver, and one of them was *substituted* instead of refused. It is now explicit, and `FiscalCadenaOriginalHardeningTest` pins the four refusals plus the fact that makes the design correct at all: the stylesheet **ignores `@Sello`**, so a sealed document states the same cadena as the seal-less one, which is the premise `L18`(d) rests on and nothing had stated in a test. (2) Nothing compared the timbre's `SelloCFD` with the `Sello` we sent, so a provider returning a different document with a self-consistent UUID pair would have been stored as if it were ours; the adapter now reads `Comprobante/@Sello` from the request, refuses a seal-less document without sending it, and refuses a timbre that echoes another seal. (3) "One instant" was argued and undetectable: `Fecha` has second precision, so a regression that read the clock twice passed the suite — and the seal would then be over a document whose `Fecha` differs from the one sent, which is an irreversible wrong document. The stamper now takes a `Clock` derived from the configured fiscal zone, and the suite supplies one that advances a second per read, so a second read fails the case.

(e) **What remains unproven, stated once and not hedged.** No CSD and no sandbox exist here, so no PAC has ever accepted a seal produced by this code: the golden `cadena original` is a change detector against the committed stylesheet, not the authority's own arithmetic. The password-protected key case pins a PBES2 envelope this tree builds, not one the SAT's tools emit. `cfdiTimbrado` is decoded as UTF-8, so `S10`'s byte identity is conditional on the provider answering UTF-8. And a timbre that states **no** `SelloCFD` is tolerated, because the authority's timbre 1.0 spells it `selloCFD` and the vendor's own committed example is such a document — the rule that matters, a *different* document with a self-consistent UUID pair, is refused.

(f) **What is deliberately outside this unit.** No `FISCAL_*` key exists in `docker/docker-compose.yml` or in `k8s/`, so the shipped stack cannot turn the provider on without a compose edit; and when it does, the CSD has to arrive as a mounted file read by an entrypoint wrapper and exported as the variable, never as a value in a file under version control — which is this repository's own rule and the reason the wiring is a unit of its own rather than four lines here. `T8`'s cancelling half is still open, and the service's `enviarSolicitudCancelacion` operation is the one that can carry a motive and a substituting folio.

(g) **Size, stated rather than discovered**: 73 files and 9087 authored lines across four commits, and the number needs its reason — 225 KB of that is the authority's own XSLT, not this tree's code. The behavioural split is `L18`(f)'s, each half is one pull request, and the 51 deletions are the seal-less and sealed entry points replacing the single one `T6` had.

**L20 (2026-11-15) — the cancellation contract, read from the vendor's own pages, and the three things it says that the timbrado half does not.** The issue is #925. The contract is `https://solucionfactible.com/sfic/capitulos/timbrado/ws-timbrado-enviarSolicitudCancelacion.jsp` plus the four example archives it links (`request_soap_Timbrado.enviarSolicitudCancelacion.zip`, `solicitudCancelacion-anexo20.zip`, `response_soap_correcto…zip`, `response_soap_error…zip`), downloaded and read rather than recalled, in the discipline `L18`(b) established. Nine decisions.

(a) **The operation is `enviarSolicitudCancelacion`, and the reason is custody — the same axis that chose the provider.** The vendor's service carries four cancellation operations and they split on who signs. `cancelar`, `cancelarBase64` and `cancelarPorNotaCredito` take `derCertCSD` + `derKeyCSD` + `contrasenaCSD`, so the **private key travels to the PAC on every call** for it to build and sign the request; `cancelarViaCSDAlmacenado` takes none because the PAC already holds a CSD, which is the custody posture `L18`(a) refused. `enviarSolicitudCancelacion` takes `usuario`, `password` and one `xs:base64Binary` `solicitudCancelacion`, and the vendor's page says of it: *«se entiende que el factura electrónica … que usted este enviando a cancelar ya vendra firmado»*. So the request is built and sealed **here**, with the CSD this tree already loads for `T7b`, and the key never leaves the process — the cancellation half reuses the sealing capability instead of inverting the posture that decided the provider.

(b) **The vendor now recommends an operation this slice does not use, and the reason is that its answer says less.** The same page opens with *«se recomienda usar el método … `enviarSolicitudCancelacionAsincrono`»*. That operation returns `CancelacionAsincronoResponse` — `status` and `mensaje` only, **no `resultados[]` and no `statusUUID`** — and its `200` means *«La solicitud de cancelación se registró exitosamente»*, which is a fact about a submission and not about a document. The synchronous operation is the only one that states a per-document outcome, and a per-document outcome is the only input from which `S7`'s row can honestly be written. The asynchronous path is the natural home of the acceptance poll (see (i)) and is named there, not smuggled in here.

(c) **The answer has three levels, not two, and `L18`(e)'s rule does not transfer to this operation.** Timbrado is mapped by the first result because process `200` can carry a refused document. Cancellation adds a level below it: `CFDIResultadoCancelacion` carries `status`, `mensaje`, `uuid` and — conditionally — **`statusUUID`**, and `statusUUID` is the SAT's verdict on the document. The vendor's own published *correct* answer proves why the two-level reading would be wrong: process `status` `200`, result `status` `200`, and `statusUUID` **`205`**, with the message *«El SAT no ha procesado la recepción de este comprobante … por favor espere al menos 24 horas»*. A mapping that stopped at the result's `200` would have recorded a cancellation the authority explicitly did not make. So the adapter reads the third level, and the only two codes that mean **cancelled** are **`201`** (*«El folio se ha cancelado con éxito»*) and **`202`** (*«El CFDI ya se había cancelado previamente»*).

(d) **`211` is a third outcome, and it writes nothing.** *«La cancelación está en proceso»* is a submitted request the receiver has not answered; `S7`'s row shape has no state for it (`ck_fiscal_document_status` is `DRAFT`/`STAMPED`/`CANCELLED` and this slice adds none), and writing `CANCELLED` on it would be the lie `V108` exists to prevent. So the port's result is a three-way sealed value — `Cancelled`, `InProcess`, `Rejected` — and the service writes a row only for the first. The endpoint answers **`202 Accepted`** with the code for `InProcess`, which is HTTP's own word for "submitted, not decided", and **no row**. The gap this leaves is stated rather than papered over: a submitted cancellation is not remembered anywhere until the acceptance poll lands, and that is (i).

(e) **The motive becomes a closed vocabulary, and that is a deliberate reversal of `V109`.** `V109` RAISES if any `CHECK` pins `cancellation_motive`, on the argument that `c_MotivoCancelacion` is "stated nowhere in this tree". The argument was sound and its premise has changed: the SAT's `cancelación.xsd` declares `<xsd:enumeration>` `01`–`04` under a **required** `Motivo` attribute, the vendor's catalogue lists the same four with their meanings and the 2022-01-01 rule that every request carries one, and **neither declares `05`**. So `V112` adds `ck_fiscal_document_cancellation_motive`, `FiscalCancellationMotive` mirrors it, and the migration test's constraint inventory grows by that one deliberate entry. `exportacion` and `tax_object` stay unconstrained, because neither has been read from a primary source — the reversal is one column wide, which is the difference between reading a catalog and guessing one.

(f) **`FolioSustitucion` is required for `01` and is *not* forbidden otherwise, and the record says the second half rather than inventing it.** The XSD makes the attribute `use="optional"` and documents it as *«requerido cuando la clave del motivo de cancelación es 01»*; no retrieved source forbids it for `02`–`04`. What the SAT rejects is a **wrong** UUID (`312`), not the attribute's presence. So the rule implemented is the one-way one — motive `01` without a substituting folio is a **400** that names the field, and a folio with another motive is accepted — and the symmetric rule nobody stated is deliberately absent.

(g) **The request is a second document, with a different signature, and it is the second capability that arrives with no new dependency.** The Anexo 20 request is `<Cancelacion xmlns="http://cancelacfd.sat.gob.mx" Fecha="…" RfcEmisor="…"><Folios><Folio UUID="…" Motivo="…" FolioSustitucion="…"/></Folios><Signature xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/09/xmldsig#">…</Signature></Cancelacion>` — an **enveloped XMLDSig**, not the `Sello` = `SHA256withRSA(cadena original)` the stamping pipeline produces. `T7b`'s sealer cannot be reused: an XML DISig is canonicalisation, a digest over the enveloped document and a `SignatureValue` over `SignedInfo`. The JDK's `javax.xml.crypto.dsig` builds it, so this is the same trade `L18`(g) made for SOAP — a named standard from the platform instead of a dependency — and `Fecha` is rendered from the same single `Clock` read and the same fiscal zone as the comprobante, because the two documents are one instant.

(h) **The example archives are the fixtures, with their provenance recorded, exactly as `T7a` did.** The vendor publishes one correct and one error answer for this operation, and they are not interchangeable with the timbrado ones: the error answer has `status` `1802` at the **process** level with a `CFDIResultadoCancelacion` whose `status` and `mensaje` are **empty**, so a mapper that reads the result first finds nothing to report. Committing both, with the URL and the digest of each file beside them, is what makes the adapter's suite a test of the vendor's contract rather than of this tree's idea of it.

(i) **What this slice deliberately leaves, named so the next one is not an excavation.** Three things: the **acceptance poll** — `211` and the receiver's 72-hour window, including `AceptacionRechazo`, which needs a state this schema does not have and is therefore a migration and a unit of its own; the **asynchronous operation** of (b), which is the natural substrate for that poll; and the **delivery wiring**, unchanged from `L19`(f) — the CSD still arrives as a mounted file read by an entrypoint wrapper, and no `FISCAL_*` key exists in the compose or in `k8s/`. The cancellation screen in the Angular app is also absent: the endpoint is the deliverable, and `life-control-app-angular` has no fiscal code at all today.

(j) **Size and slices, declared before the work because the last unit's size was discovered.** The cancelling half is four reviewable slices, chained, each one a pull request of about 400 authored lines: **`T8a`** the motive vocabulary (`V112` + `FiscalCancellationMotive` + the constraint-inventory entry); **`T8b`** the Anexo 20 request and its XMLDSig sealer, with the vendor's own example as the known-answer test; **`T8c`** the cancellation port and the Solución Factible adapter, with the two published answers as fixtures; **`T8d`** the service, the cancelled row and the endpoint. The units split exactly where the dependencies do — `T8b` and `T8c` are independent of each other and both depend only on `T8a` — so the chain is a dependency statement and not a size budget applied after the fact.
